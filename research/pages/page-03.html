The mechanism

How credits work

1 credit = $0.10. Top-ups sold in blocks of 100–10,000 at that rate. There is no separate overage rate — when you run out, the AI simply stops replying until credits are added, manually or via auto-recharge against a threshold.

Their stated rule: "One AI response = one credit charge," regardless of how many messages the customer fired off — rapid incoming messages are batched into a single reply. That's a fair design. Part 4 shows how the reality differs.

The margin analysis

What a reply costs them

Using current published API pricing, and modelling one reply at ~8,000 input tokens with ~150 output. A DM bot is the ideal caching workload — the playbook and FAQ prefix are identical on every single reply, and a cache read costs ~10% of the input rate.

Look at their top plan: Agency Unlimited is $248.50/month billed yearly, and includes 2,500 credits — a face value of $250. On paper the plan is free if you value credits at list price. That tells you plainly how far above cost the credit price sits.

They then let agencies do the same thing one level down. We buy credits at $0.10 and set a per-client markup rate on each sub-account — their own example has a client burning 0.5 credits per action while our pool pays 0.2. The spread is applied automatically, with no reconciliation on our side.

Why BYOK exists: it's the pressure valve for buyers who can do this arithmetic. Rather than lose them, DM Champ lets them pay Anthropic directly and keeps the platform subscription.

Time-sensitive · 1 October 2026

Meta moved from conversation-based to per-delivered-message pricing on 1 July 2025. Today, service messages — the AI's replies inside the 24-hour customer window — are free. From 1 October 2026 Meta charges per message at each market's rate. Rates publish 1 September 2026.
