Buy DM Champ or build it

Our boss asked whether we should create a product like DM Champ, or whether theirs is cheap enough to just use. Theirs is cheaper — by roughly an order of magnitude — and the reasons why are worth understanding before we commit either way.

Recommendation

Resell theirs. Don't build — yet.

Unlimited white-label reselling costs $2,982 a year. Building a genuine equivalent is a 12–24 month project for two or more developers. Even at the most optimistic build cost, we'd need five-plus years of subscription to break even — before counting maintenance, which never stops.

Building becomes the right call only if the goal changes: if we want to own a product to sell rather than a service to deliver. That's a strategy decision with a two-year horizon, not a way to save money.

Settle this before anything else

Inside the live account there is no agency section, no sub-account tools, and no white-label controls — yet the site is fully branded as FounderGrowth.AI. There's also no monthly credit grant and no card on file.

That points at one of two very different situations: either this workspace is a sub-account inside someone else's agency — in which case we are the client, not the reseller, and none of the reselling economics are available to us yet — or the boss holds a lifetime deal and configured white-label from a separate agency-level login. Everything downstream depends on which it is.

The one-paragraph version

What we're actually looking at

DM Champ is not a website builder. It's a white-label AI sales agent — an AI that reads and replies to customer messages on WhatsApp, Instagram DMs, Messenger, Telegram, SMS, email and web chat, qualifies the lead, handles objections and books the call with no human involved. Agencies rebrand the entire platform under their own domain and resell it at whatever price they like.

Clearing this up first

foundergrowth.ai is our boss's white-labeled DM Champ instance. There is no DM Champ branding on it anywhere — that's the product working exactly as designed, not a separate thing he built. Every agency customer gets their own version of that.

The five findings that matter

What the research turned up

It costs about twice the advertised rate

Every AI action is billed separately — tool calls, name and email extraction, daily summaries, follow-ups. One real conversation produced eight charges for four visible replies. Knowledge-base training costs 1 credit per page on top.

See part 3 and part 4

The cheap AI option isn't available to us

Bring-your-own-key only zeroes credits on the Pro tier — and there is no API-key field anywhere in our account. It needs an Agency plan. Our real cost floor today is Max at 0.25 credits per billed action.

See part 4 and part 7

The hard part is untested

Only the website chat widget is connected. WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger and SMS are all disconnected — the channels that need Meta approval and that reviewers complain most about.

See part 4

Switching to ChatGPT wouldn't help

Any raw API is 10–200× cheaper than DM Champ's credits, but the entire spread between the cheapest and priciest AI API is ~$7,700/year against a $30K–$150K build. It optimises the line item that was already cheap.

See part 7

What to do

A staged plan

01
Now — next 3 to 6 months

Resell on the account we already have

Onboard three to five real clients under foundergrowth.ai. Learn the operational reality: how long setup truly takes, how good the AI is on our clients' actual conversations, and where it embarrasses us. Cost is essentially the subscription already paid.

02
Month six — decide with evidence

Revisit only on a specific trigger

We pass roughly 30 paying clients with a proven model; or we hit a hard product limit they refuse to fix; or the boss commits to a product bet rather than a service business. Absent one of those, keep reselling.

03
If at all

Build the thin layer, not the platform

Even then, don't rebuild the infrastructure. Build what differentiates us — our own vertical playbooks, our own API integrations, our own onboarding and reporting — on top of theirs. Their Custom Functions already let their bot call our APIs mid-conversation, so a safari agent could quote live availability from our Nuxt backend without us building anything.

This week, regardless of the decision

  1. Establish whether we're the agency or somebody else's client. If this workspace is a sub-account, we can't resell from it and the plan needs rewriting. Nothing else matters until this is answered.
  2. Ask what it takes to get bring-your-own-key. On Pro it makes replies roughly five times cheaper than the Max tier we're running now.
  3. Connect one Meta channel on a test account. It's the only untested part of the stack and the one most likely to cost us weeks.
  4. Look at why 14 replies produced a 0% rate, and move the agent off "Fast" response speed — the UI itself warns that setting lowers reply quality.
  5. Change the account password. It's been passed around in chat and looks like a default.
  6. Diarise 1 September to check Meta's published WhatsApp rates before quoting any client.
Time-sensitive · 1 October 2026

Meta is changing WhatsApp pricing. Service messages — the AI's replies inside the 24-hour customer window — are free today and become charged per message on 1 October. Rates publish 1 September, about a week from now.

This hits every platform equally, including one we build ourselves, so it doesn't favour either side of the decision — but it changes what we must charge clients. UAE rates are steep (~$0.03–$0.08/message versus India's ~$0.001–$0.009).
