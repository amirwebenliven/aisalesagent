Build or buy the platform

Scope

Four systems, not one

The part that feels like the product is the smallest slice of the work. That asymmetry is the whole trap.

1 · The AI agent layer

4–8 weeks

Prompt orchestration, knowledge retrieval, conversation memory with summarisation, tool calling, human handoff, the URL-to-playbook crawler. Genuinely tractable with a modern LLM API. The realism tricks — reply delays, message splitting, simulated typing — are simple to copy once you know they matter. This is the easy part.

2 · Omnichannel messaging

3–6 months

WhatsApp Cloud API, Instagram Messaging, Messenger, Telegram, Twilio SMS, IMAP/Gmail/Outlook, a web chat widget. Each has its own webhook contract, message format, media handling, delivery receipts, rate limits and 24-hour-window rules — and Meta changes them constantly. Permanent maintenance.

3 · Multi-tenant white-label

3–5 months

Everything in part 5: tenant isolation, per-tenant theming, custom domains with automated certificate renewal, impersonation with audit trails, a credit ledger with per-client markup and atomic double-deduction, Stripe billing, snapshots. Security-sensitive, tedious, invisible until it breaks.

4 · The application

3–6 months

Unified inbox, contact CRM, campaign builder, booking with calendar sync, pipeline, analytics, REST API, webhooks, admin tooling.

Total to genuine parity: 12–24 months with two or more developers. A narrow MVP — WhatsApp plus web chat, one AI agent, a basic inbox, no white-label — is maybe 3–4 months for two developers, and would not yet be resellable.

The real barrier

The part code can't solve

This is the constraint most people underestimate.

For a single business using WhatsApp Business API, onboarding is fast: 3–10 business days end to end — 2–4 days for Business Verification, hours for WABA and number setup, 24–48 hours for the first template approval.

For a platform serving other businesses' accounts — which is exactly what we'd be — it's a different process:

  • Instagram messaging needs Advanced Access to instagram_manage_messages, plus instagram_basic, pages_manage_metadata and pages_show_list — granted only through App Review on a verified Meta Business portfolio.
  • Meta's team actively tests the app's functionality and audits policy compliance. A missing or non-working webhook callback URL is a standard rejection.
  • Rejections are common and each round trip costs weeks.
  • Ongoing compliance risk is permanent — using the human_agent tag for automated bot replies is explicitly prohibited and is cited as one of the fastest ways to lose API access entirely.

DM Champ has already been through it and carries that risk on their account, not ours. That is a substantial, unpriced part of what the subscription buys.

Note this cuts both ways: reviewers report DM Champ's own Instagram connection flow is fragile — one user spent three days failing to connect. Meta integration is painful even when someone else has done the approval work. And per part 4, we haven't tested it at all yet.

 Build cost varies hugely by region and seniority. Neither figure includes the opportunity cost of those developers not building anything else.

The range on "buy" is the honest one, and it depends entirely on the BYOK question in part 4:

  • With bring-your-own-key on the Pro tier: ~$2,982 platform + ~$3,000 direct to the model provider = ~$6,000/year for unlimited clients.
  • Without it, on the Max tier we're running today: ~$2,982 + ~$30,000 in credits at 100 clients = ~$33,000/year.

Even the worse case beats building. At $33,000/year we'd still need four-plus years to equal a single year of building — and that ignores maintenance, which never stops.

The detail that settles it

The usual case for building is that per-message platform fees explode at volume. Bring-your-own-key is the escape hatch DM Champ built to prevent exactly that. Plug in our own key on the Pro tier and AI replies cost zero credits; we pay the model provider directly at cost, and marginal cost per client collapses.

Getting access to that setting is worth more than any plan negotiation — and it's currently the single highest-value open question.

If we do build

Don't start from zero

Chatwoot is a mature open-source omnichannel inbox — live chat, email, WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, Telegram — free to self-host, with automations, contact profiles and a built-in AI agent. It would eliminate most of system 2 and much of system 4 above, plausibly halving the timeline.

What it does not give us: the multi-tenant white-label billing layer, the Meta App Review approval, or the URL-to-agent onboarding. Those are precisely the expensive, risky parts. It changes the estimate; it doesn't change the recommendation.

The risk of buying

Platform dependency

Name it before committing

DM Champ is 3 people, ~$330K ARR, bootstrapped, founded 2023. We'd be putting our client-facing brand and our clients' live sales conversations on it. If they're acquired, pivot or fold, every white-labeled client breaks simultaneously — and those clients think the product is ours.

Mitigations, all cheap:

  • Hold our own client contracts; never let DM Champ be the contracting party
  • Export contacts and conversation data on a schedule — Data Export exists and they have a documented DPA
  • Keep the AI playbooks in our own repo as the source of truth, not only in their UI
  • Prefer Managed billing mode so client payment relationships stay ours
  • Use our own model API key where possible
  • Stay on monthly billing until the offer is validated

On the other side of the ledger: bootstrapped and profitable at three people is a stable shape. They're not burning VC money chasing a pivot, and reviewers consistently praise the team shipping fixes within days.

The exception

When building would make sense

The financial case is weak. These are the non-financial cases, and they're legitimate:

  1. We want a product to sell, not a service to deliver. A SaaS we own is an asset with enterprise value; a reseller arrangement is not. If the boss's real ambition is to build DM Champ's business, not use their software, everything above is beside the point — but it should be an explicit, funded product bet with a 12–24 month horizon, not a cost-saving exercise.
  2. A vertical they'll never serve. A safari/tourism agent wired into our own Nuxt API — live availability, real itineraries, real pricing — could be genuinely differentiated. But their Custom Functions already let their bot call our API mid-conversation, so we can likely get 80% of that without building anything.
  3. Platform risk becomes unacceptable.
  4. Data or regulatory requirements we can't meet on their infrastructure.
