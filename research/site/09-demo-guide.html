The mental model

Four layers, and only four

Almost every question about this product answers itself once these four boxes are clear. Channels are how messages arrive. The agent is the brain. The knowledge base is what it knows. Actions are what it can do besides talk.

The question everyone asks

The website is not "connected"

This is the bit that surprises people

The AI does not visit altaherchemicals.com when someone asks a question. The site was crawled once, and the crawler turned its content into a library of question-and-answer pairs. The AI answers out of that stored library.

In our account right now, under AI Studio → Knowledge Base:

59Pages read from altaherchemicals.com
129FAQs the AI generated from them
25×Times the most-used FAQ has been drawn on

Every FAQ is editable, and each shows a usage count, so you can see which answers are actually earning their place. You can also write FAQs by hand for things that were never on the website — pricing rules, lead times, "we don't ship there".

Why this matters commercially

  • Change the website and the AI won't know. Someone has to press Refresh on the source to re-crawl. This is a real operational task we'd owe every client, and worth charging for.
  • It costs about 1 credit per page to ingest. 59 pages ≈ 59 credits ≈ $5.90 of setup for this one client.
  • Bad website in, bad answers out. If a client's site is thin or out of date, the AI inherits that. Auditing the site is part of onboarding, not an afterthought.

You can prove the link live: FAQ #45 in the library says Al Taher has "over 20 years of experience" across the GCC, and FAQ #50 lists "conventional and catalytic bright chrome". Both phrases came straight back out of the AI in this afternoon's real conversation, below.

The shortcut worth knowing

WhatsApp Web is the demo weapon. It needs no Meta approval, no Business API, no number purchase — just a QR scan, and a real WhatsApp number is live in about two minutes. That's how our number got connected today.

Its trade-off: each number warms up over about 60 days, with a rising daily cap. Ours is on day 1 — 50 messages/day, and cold outreach is disabled until it matures. Inbound conversations work fine, which is all a demo needs. For a client in production at volume, the Business API is still the right answer.

Where the agent meets the channel

An agent is assigned to channels, not tied to one. Our agents list says plainly: "Tia | ATC answers all 2 connected channels." One brain, every inbox. Connect Instagram tomorrow and the same agent covers it with no extra setup — that's the whole pitch in one sentence.

Before the call

Two things that could spoil the demo

Check both an hour before

Credits are down to 39. Every AI action — each reply, each tool call, each name or email extraction, each daily summary — bills separately, so real usage runs about 0.5 credits per visible reply. That's roughly 70–80 replies left. A live demo plus a chatty client could exhaust it mid-meeting, and when credits hit zero the AI simply stops replying with no warning to the person messaging. Top up first.

The number is on day 1 of a 60-day warm-up. 50 messages/day and no cold outreach. Fine for a demo; say it out loud before the client discovers it, and frame it as a WhatsApp anti-spam protection rather than a product limit — which is what it is.

For the meeting

An honest pros and cons

What's genuinely good

  • Setup really is fast. Paste a URL, get a working agent. 59 pages became 129 answers with no prompt engineering.
  • It doesn't read like a bot. Split messages, typing delays, clarifying questions instead of guessing.
  • It knows what it doesn't know — the food-contact moment above.
  • One agent covers every channel, and a human can take over in the same inbox.
  • WhatsApp with no Meta approval via QR — genuinely rare.
  • It can call our own systems through Custom Functions, so answers can come from live data, not just the crawl.

What to be upfront about

  • Credit costs run ~2× the headline rate because every tool call and extraction bills separately.
  • The knowledge base goes stale until someone re-crawls. It is not live.
  • Instagram and Messenger need Meta App Review — weeks, and rejections are common. Untested on our account.
  • Contacts can show as "Unknown Unknown" — a Meta limitation, but it looks unpolished in the inbox.
  • WhatsApp Web numbers are rate-limited for 60 days.
  • The vendor is three people. Bootstrapped and profitable, but small.
The other option

Would MeshAPI let us build this instead?

MeshAPI is a model gateway, not a product. One OpenAI-compatible key gives access to 1,000+ models across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Mistral and DeepSeek, with smart routing, automatic failover between providers, response caching and per-request logs. You change two lines — the base URL and the key.

For an India-based team it's a genuinely sensible choice: pay in rupees by UPI, from ₹1, one balance instead of a card on file with four foreign providers, and no platform fee on top of model cost.

But be clear what it replaces

Look back at the diagram. MeshAPI supplies one part of one box — the model behind the agent. It provides no WhatsApp connection, no Instagram, no inbox, no contacts or CRM, no knowledge-base crawler, no follow-up scheduling, no multi-client white-label layer.

Those are the months of work. The model API was always the cheap, easy part — at realistic volume the model bill is single-digit dollars per month, while hosting and infrastructure are ten to twenty times that.

So MeshAPI doesn't shorten a build from months to weeks; it makes one small line item cheaper and easier to pay for. If we do build, use it. It is not a reason to build.
